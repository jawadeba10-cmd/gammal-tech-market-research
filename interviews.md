# User Research Report: Preventive Healthcare Habits

## Executive Summary

I conducted three interviews to validate the core value proposition of our AI-driven preventive healthcare system. The main goal was to identify if people are willing to shift from reactive to proactive health management.

## Interview 1: The Student (High School)

* Participant Profile: Male, 16, Tripoli, Lebanon.

* Preventive Habits: Plays football twice a week but has no specific diet.

* Key Insight: Does not think about long-term health risks (chronic diseases). 

* Reaction to AI: Would use the app only if it provides immediate benefits, such as "optimised energy for exams" or "muscle recovery tips," rather than long-term disease warnings.

* Friction: Finds manual data entry "exhausting."

## Interview 2: The Working Professional

* Participant Profile: Female, 34, Office Employee.

* Preventive Habits: Takes vitamins occasionally; tries to limit sugar but often fails due to stress.

* Key Insight: Has a family history of diabetes and worries about it, but doesn't know how to track "hidden" sugars in restaurant meals.

* Reaction to AI: Very positive toward the "early warning" system. Stated: "If an AI told me my risk is increasing based on my actual lunch, I would change my behaviour immediately."

* Friction: Privacy concerns regarding sharing data with restaurants.

## Interview 3: The Retired Professional

* Participant Profile: Male, 62, Retired Engineer.

* Preventive Habits: Daily walks and regular blood pressure checks.

* Key Insight: Already practices preventive care but finds it difficult to coordinate information between his doctor, his diet, and his physical activity.

* Reaction to AI: Interested in the "Centralised Connection" (Doctor-Gym-AI). He sees the value in his doctor having access to his real-time habit data.

* Friction: Technology barrier; the interface must be extremely simple and clear.

## Synthesis of Findings (Product Requirements)

1.  Automation (Data Engineering/AI): Manual logging is a deterrent. This healthcare platform must focus on automated data collection from wearables and restaurant APIs.

2.  Trust & Transparency (QA/UX): Users require clear evidence for why the AI is giving a specific warning to ensure "User Trust."

3.  Value Alignment: The platform must tailor its messaging: "Performance" for younger users and "Longevity/Risk Management" for older users.

## Conclusion

These interviews confirm a significant market gap for a system that connects lifestyle data in medical risk detection. Applying these insights to ensure the platform addresses these specific user frictions would be a success.
